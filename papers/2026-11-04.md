### The African swine fever virus I73R protein is a multifunctional Zα domain that mediates both Z-binding-dependent and -independent biological functions through induction of phase separation
**게재일**: 2026-11-03 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1008/9010736?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract African swine fever virus (ASFV) I73R encodes a protein consisting only of a Zalpha (Zα) domain. Here, we studied its biochemical properties. Complementary approaches demonstrated that I73R is a multifunctional Zα domain that combines Z-binding, B-to-Z conversion, and liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS) induction activities. I73R proved to be a better converter than human ADAR1 Zα, and it retained conversion activity with Y48I mutation of its Z-binding site. Next, we investigated I73R biological functions, leading to the following findings: (i) Ectopic expression of I73R or mutated I73R with deficient Z-binding both inhibited arsenite-induced stress granules to a similar extent, and none of these proteins localized within these organelles. (ii) I73R formed cytosolic condensates with dsRNA, Z-DNA, and Z-RNA in ASFV-infected cells. (iii) Rescuing of ASFV I73R deletion by sequences encoding natural, mutated, and chimeric forms of Zα domains demonstrated that I73R mediated both Z-binding-dependent and -independent biological functions through induction of LLPS. This conclusion was further supported by the effect of the LLPS inhibitor 1,6-hexanediol on the replication of ASFV I73R recombinant strains. This study further demonstrates the functional diversity that exists among Zα domains. It unravels new outlooks targeting Zα domain-mediated LLPS for the development of pharmaceutical molecules able to regulate viral and cellular Zα domain-containing proteins.

### Varied Ensembles of Long-range ChROmatin interactions drive chromatin compartmentalization
**게재일**: 2026-11-03 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1082/9010723?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract The chromatin components that are involved in organizing eukaryotic genomes into active and inactive compartments remain unclear. In analysing viable Caenorhabditis elegans mutants lacking different chromatin-regulatory proteins, we found that the HP1-type factor HPL-2 and the MBT-domain protein LIN-61 jointly contribute to chromatin compartmentalization. Genomic regions bound by both proteins and their cognate histone mark H3 lysine 9 dimethylation (H3K9me2) form broad interfaces of 30–50 kilobases that engage in combinatorial interactions, which we term ‘Varied Ensembles of Long-range ChROmatin interactions’ (VELCROs). Remarkably, VELCROs are defined by high density of H3K9me2, HPL-2, and LIN-61 sites, but not by the intensity of their individual peaks. Mutants that display defective chromatin compartmentalization are impaired in VELCROs. Polymer simulations show that VELCROs can drive a phase transition that segregates B from A chromatin compartments depending on peak densities. Our work identifies VELCROs as stochastic ensembles of interactions constrained within defined chromatin hubs that shape genome compartmentalization.

### Monomeric ZRANB3 drives replication fork reversal
**게재일**: 2026-11-03 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1034/9010722?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Replication fork reversal is a central response to replication stress, yet how a SNF2 translocase coordinates the distinct mechanical steps required for this reaction remains elusive. Here, we define the mechanism by which ZRANB3 catalyzes fork reversal using biochemical, biophysical, and single-molecule approaches. ZRANB3 binds fork DNA as a monomer at the junction, establishing the minimal functional unit required for activity. Mechanistically, ZRANB3 combines ATP-dependent localized duplex opening with ATP-independent strand annealing to facilitate four-way junction formation, followed by directional branch migration that supports long-range fork reversal. Single-molecule fluorescence analyses further demonstrate that monomeric ZRANB3 is sufficient to drive fork reversal. Together, these findings define how ZRANB3 integrates distinct DNA remodeling activities within a single enzyme to catalyze fork reversal.
