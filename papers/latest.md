### Structural basis for coupled DNA replication and uracil repair by a family of DNA polymerases from phages with T-modified genomes
**게재일**: 2026-10-26 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1018/9001953?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Replicative DNA polymerases (DNAPs) play a critical role in genome duplication, ensuring the accurate transmission of genetic information in all domains of life. DNAPs are also essential for DNA viruses, as well as bacteriophages, where a uracil-DNA glycosylase (UDG) is often encoded in trans or is present in the DNAP gene. Here, we identify a new group of A-family DNAPs in phages that are fused to an active UDG domain and contain other large insertions. Several members of this subfamily, including phages SP-15 and YerA41, are known to be hypermodified on their thymidine base through glycosylation of a linker attached to the C5 position of thymine. We determined the atomic cryo-EM structures for two of its members in various active functional and conformational states, from YerA41 and phiLo phages. The structures reveal how these large DNAPs function as replicative DNAPs, and how they can recognize dU bases ahead of the replication fork through their UDG domain, generating abasic sites that are preferentially bypassed through dA incorporation. Such dU errors would need to be eliminated to preserve phage genome integrity. Additionally, we report the co-occurrence of both an X-family DNAP and a DNA ligase in the corresponding phage genomes and show that both enzymes can execute the final steps of base excision repair most probably on-the-fly during replication, revealing a DNA-replication-coupled repair process. The structures of these large polymerases can serve as a model of other large platform DNAPs that can recruit additional enzymatic activities necessary for phage or virus survival.

### Neuronal DNA repair mediates systemic mitochondrial maintenance through acetylcholine signaling
**게재일**: 2026-10-26 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag976/9001951?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract DNA repair defects such as nucleotide excision repair (NER) mutations can lead to progeroid syndromes characterized by growth retardation and accelerated aging with distinct tissue type specific pathologies. DNA-damage-induced dysfunction of specific cell types can impair the organism’s overall function. It is unknown whether, in reverse, DNA repair in specific tissues can have non-cell-autonomous consequences on other tissues carrying DNA damage. To explore the organismal consequences of cell-type-specific DNA repair, we restricted NER activity to specific tissues and monitored consequences on organismal health in Caenorhabditis elegans . Unexpectedly, we observed that tissue-specific DNA repair is sufficient to improve overall DNA damage resistance. Strikingly, NER activity restricted to neurons only is sufficient to provide similar overall DNA damage resistance as whole-body NER in almost all tissues tested. We show that this systemic DNA damage resilience is mediated by neuronal acetylcholine signaling that promotes the maintenance of mitochondrial activity throughout the organism.
