### Systematic engineering of synthetic minimal promoters for tunable transgene expression in mammalian cells
**게재일**: 2026-11-04 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1052/9011590?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Fine-tuning transgene levels is crucial in fundamental and applied research. Native promoters are broadly utilized to regulate transgene expression. However, their large size and structural complexity hinder predictable tuning, constrain compatibility with viral vectors with limited capacity, and increase the risk of homologous recombination. Synthetic promoters are short de novo sequences, typically consisting of an enhancer region, which is positioned upstream of a minimal promoter that determines transcription initiation efficiency. Enhancer optimization typically yields coarse activation shifts, alters response curves, and requires case-by-case optimization. Synthetic minimal promoter architecture enables fine-tuning that is potentially compatible with any enhancer region. In this study, we systematically optimized minimal promoter sequences, core element composition, and regulatory motif sequences. Our findings support a modular model of minimal promoter function in which cis- regulatory elements from different minimal promoters can be mixed, altered, added, or deleted to yield predictable expression outcomes. We shortened and thereby enhanced the efficiency of known minimal promoters, engineered modular tunable minimal promoters, and generated candidate regulatory elements spanning 7–8 nucleotides that drive efficient transcription when combined with artificial ${5^{\prime}}$ untranslated regions (5'UTRs) that function as an additional regulatory layer. This framework broadens the utility of synthetic promoters for next-generation applications.

### DNA mimic helical foldamers with up to 100 units: synthesis and recognition of a multiprotein complex
**게재일**: 2026-11-04 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag974/9011589?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract DNA mimic foldamers (DMFOs) are helically folded aromatic oligoamides that mimic the shape and charge distribution of double-stranded B-DNA. DMFOs consist of an alternation of two amino acid monomers, M and Q. Here, we introduce a new synthetic approach of (MQ) n oligomers on solid phase that enabled the production of sequences as long as a 100mer, formally equivalent to a 50-base pair DNA duplex. The approach takes advantage of dimeric Fmoc-MQ-OH as building blocks. Three such macromonomers were synthesized using Q and M units bearing different side chains. Solid phase synthesis protocols were validated in all series. Helix handedness was shown to be determined by a single chiral residue. The purified long oligomers were analyzed by reversed phase HPLC, mass spectrometry, circular dichroism, and nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy for their purity, identity, and helix-handedness bias. Clusters of foldamers forming in the presence of Ca 2+ could be visualized by cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM). Lastly, a 72mer DMFO was shown to bind to the A-module of the chromatin remodeling complex INO80 slightly tighter than a comparable DNA duplex. A cryo-EM structure of the complex at a resolution of 3.59 Å showed that the DMFO occupies the A-module DNA binding region.

### Detection and quantification of DNA double-strand breaks by genomic and imaging methods: a critical appraisal of capabilities and limitations
**게재일**: 2026-11-04 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1035/9011588?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) are the most genotoxic form of DNA damage. Unrepaired DSBs may cause loss of chromosome fragments at mitosis, while incorrectly repaired DSBs can cause chromosome aberrations and other adverse effects, leading to cell cycle arrest, senescence, cell death, or neoplastic transformation. Detecting and quantifying DSBs is important in studies of genome instability, aging, and cancer. Methods of detecting and quantifying DSBs fall into two broad categories: imaging-based methods, used to detect and localize DNA breaks in individual cells, and genomic methods, a new category utilizing massively parallel DNA sequencing to map DSBs genome-wide in a cell population. Imaging and genomic methods yield fundamentally different information. This review focuses on a critical evaluation of the capabilities and limitations of genomic and imaging methods for the detection and quantification of DSBs. It explains why the class of genomic methods, where quantification is based on counting the detected unique DSBs and the number of cells in the sample, results in inaccurate assessment of the average number of DSBs per cell and how this problem can be solved. We also discuss complementarity of genomic and imaging methods and potential directions of their future development.

Here, we combine selective killing modules with optimized conjugative vectors to target antibiotic-resistant pathogens and clinically relevant resistance plasmids. ### CFDP1 is required for histone variant H2A.Z deposition by the human SRCAP chromatin remodeling complex
**게재일**: 2026-11-04 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1099/9011585?rss=1)

We find that CFDP1 weakly interacts with SRCAP-C in a salt concentration-dependent manner and is essential for H2A.Z dimer exchange by SRCAP-C. We show that CFDP1 stimulates the basal ATPase activity of reconstituted SRCAP-C, suggesting a requirement for CFDP1 in regulating intrinsic catalytic ATPase activity. Additionally, CFDP1 deficiency in human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs) leads to a genome-wide reduction of H2A.Z, H3K27me3, and H3K4me3 deposition, accompanied by the upregulation of developmental genes normally marked by these modifications. Taken together, our results provide mechanistic insights into how CFDP1 regulates histone variant H2A.Z deposition by SRCAP-C. Given mutations in the SRCAP gene cause Floating-Harbor syndrome, a rare, dominant developmental disorder, our study provides an additional link between craniofacial development and SRCAP-mediated H2A.Z deposition.
