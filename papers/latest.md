### Metal-ion coordination drives active-site remodeling for backsplicing in circularly permuted group II introns
**게재일**: 2026-10-29 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1053/9004261?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Circularly permuted (CP) group II introns are self-splicing ribozymes that undergo a sequential branching and backsplicing reaction to yield branched introns and circular exons (circRNAs). While the initial branching step is structurally characterized, the mechanistic principles that govern the subsequent large-scale active-site remodeling and stringent metal-ion specificity required for backsplicing remain poorly understood. Here, we present a series of high-resolution cryo-EM structures of the CP group II intron from Gluconacetobacter diazotrophicus PAL5 ( Gdi -1), capturing the ribozyme at discrete stages along the branching -to- backsplicing pathway under distinct ionic conditions. Comparative analyses under K + /Mg 2+ , K + /Ca 2+ , and Cs + /Mg 2+ conditions demonstrate that productive backsplicing relies on ion-specific coordination networks that govern 3′ splice site (3′SS) docking and catalytic center formation, revealing the precise metal-ion-coordinated conformational rearrangements and dynamic splice-site exchange mechanisms. Crucially, we identify a previously unrecognized, highly selective monovalent cation-binding site, K3, that structurally couples the γ–γ′ interaction to reassembly of the heteronuclear four-metal core, thereby promoting productive second-step active-site formation. Together, these findings establish a structural basis for metal-ion-dependent active-site remodeling during group II intron catalysis and provide mechanistic insights into metal-ion selectivity in circRNA biogenesis.

### The Mycobacterium tuberculosis Ku C-terminus orchestrates LigD activity through domain-specific interactions
**게재일**: 2026-10-29 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1036/9004259?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Bacterial non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) is a key DNA double-strand break (DSB) repair pathway that relies on the Ku–LigD complex to alleviate genomic instability. The Mycobacterium tuberculosis Ku C-terminus has been highlighted for its role in LigD recruitment to DSBs and stimulation of LigD ligase activity. However, it remains unclear how the Ku C-terminus interacts with and potentially influences other LigD activities. Here, we combine NMR spectroscopy, structural modelling, and mutational analysis to define the interaction interface between the Ku C-terminus and LigD. We identify critical residues in Ku (E246, V248, S258, K260, and N266) and the LigD polymerase (D162, V194, R198) and ligase (D522, K579, L580) domains that mediate this interaction. Functional assays reveal that Ku stimulates LigD ligase activity through contacts with both polymerase and ligase domains, while Ku attenuates template-dependent polymerase activity, contrasting previous studies in Pseudomonas aeruginosa homologs. Disrupting the Ku–LigD interface, either through Ku or LigD mutations, abolishes ligase stimulation and restores polymerase activity, highlighting a dual regulatory mechanism. Our data supports a model where Ku’s C-terminal region forms a bipartite interface with LigD to balance repair activity. These findings provide mechanistic insight into the Ku–LigD repair mechanism and uncovers species-specific differences in bacterial NHEJ.

We validated our approach using simulated grafting combinations and compared to other methods. When applying mobileRNA to real data, we find poor overlap to existing mobile mRNA annotations, consistent with recent studies that question the reliability of previously reported mobile mRNAs. Introducing a genome mappability index as a criterion for the likely success of detecting mobile RNAs, we find that SNP-based or two-step mapping approaches are inherently limited due to errors and poor mappability compared to cross-species grafting with large evolutionary distances.

### The 7SK small nuclear ribonucleoprotein links the cell responses to transcription and replication stress by promoting replication fork reversal and homologous recombination
**게재일**: 2026-10-29 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1021/9004257?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract The 7SK small nuclear ribonucleoprotein (7SK-snRNP) releases positive transcription elongation factor b to sustain RNA polymerase II (RNAPII) activity during transcription stress. Many conditions that block transcription also cause replication stress, and 7SK-snRNP components have potential roles in transcription–replication conflicts and double-strand break (DSB) repair. Here, we investigate roles of 7SK-snRNP components in replication stress responses. We report that 7SK-snRNP promotes replication fork slowing in response to agents that cause both replication and transcription stress. Our data suggest that 7SK-snRNP facilitates RAD51-mediated replication fork reversal rather than transcription–replication conflicts. 7SK-snRNP also promotes RAD51 recruitment to replication-associated DSBs or post-replicative gaps under conditions of transcription stress and supports homologous recombination at direct DSBs. In contrast, 7SK-snRNP components are not required for RAD51 recruitment in response to hydroxyurea. Our data support that LARP7 functions during replication stress independently of its interaction with BRCA1, and that LARP7 and HEXIM1 promote survival of replication stress. 7SK-snRNP components are not recruited to stressed replication forks, and RNAPII inhibition phenocopies loss of these proteins. Our data support a model where 7SK-snRNP modulation of RNAPII activity helps facilitate several RAD51 functions under transcription stress conditions, thereby connecting the cell responses to transcription and replication stress.
