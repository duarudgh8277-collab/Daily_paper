## Nucleic Acids Research

### An RNA-targeting ADP-ribosyltransferase toxin uncouples mRNA stability from productive translation
**게재일**: 2026-11-14 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1177/9051728?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Messenger RNA (mRNA) modifications regulate transcript stability and translation efficiency across diverse biological systems, but the consequences of toxin-mediated mRNA modification in bacteria remain poorly understood. RhsP2 was recently identified as an RNA-targeting interbacterial ADP-ribosyltransferase toxin, but whether mRNAs are physiological substrates remained unknown. Here, we show that RhsP2 targets mRNA coding sequences and in doing so prevents their efficient translation. eCLIP-seq reveals preferential association of RhsP2 with UAAG-enriched motifs, and biochemical analyses showed that these sequences are preferred substrates for ribose 2′-OH ADP-ribosylation. Unexpectedly, modified mRNAs were broadly stabilized despite impaired protein synthesis, demonstrating that RhsP2 uncouples transcript stability from productive translation. Monosome- and disome-seq analyses show that RhsP2-modified mRNAs induce ribosome stalling and collision, and in vitro translation reactions demonstrate that ADP-ribosylation of mRNAs is sufficient to block translation elongation. Together, these findings establish mRNA ADP-ribosylation as a mechanism of toxin-mediated translational sabotage in which chemically modified transcripts accumulate while disrupting protein synthesis.

### Structure of a dopamine-binding RNA aptamer reveals metal-mediated ligand recognition
**게재일**: 2026-11-14 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1189/9051727?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract The selection of small-molecule-binding RNA aptamers enables the creation of ligand-responsive RNA tools, yet most aptamers fail to operate reliably across diverse environments. Scaffolded selection addresses this limitation by preserving the tertiary architecture of a riboswitch scaffold while driving the evolution of a new ligand-binding pocket. Using the purine riboswitch aptamer, we previously generated dopamine-binding aptamers. Here, we report the crystal structures of two representative variants in their apo and dopamine-bound states to define how they recognize ligand while maintaining scaffold integrity. The structures demonstrate that scaffolded selection enforces global fold conservation and retains the defining tertiary interactions of the parental riboswitch. Local remodeling, triggered by deletions acquired during selection, rewires the three-way junction to build extensive interaction networks that host the dopamine-binding pocket. A deeply buried potassium ion anchors ligand recognition, while the RNA engages the catechol ring through stacking and hydrogen bonding interactions. Structure probing shows minimal conformational changes upon ligand binding, indicating that the aptamers adopt a largely preorganized fold. The balance between structural stability and local plasticity expands the capacity of a single RNA fold to recognize chemically distinct ligands and positions scaffolded selection as a powerful platform for engineering robust RNA-based sensing and regulatory devices.
