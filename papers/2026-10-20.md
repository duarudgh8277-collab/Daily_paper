### Integrative analysis reveals extensive interactions among C2H2 zinc finger proteins at chromatin loop anchors
**게재일**: 2026-10-19 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1004/8937191?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Cys2-His2-zinc-finger proteins (C2H2-ZFPs) form the largest class of human transcription factors, yet their potential roles in higher-order genome organization remain poorly defined. To date, only a few C2H2-ZFPs, e.g. CTCF, have been implicated in chromatin organization. Here, we systematically examined the global relationship between C2H2-ZFPs and long-range chromatin interactions (LRIs). By integrating ChIP-seq datasets for 216 C2H2-ZFPs with genome-wide chromatin interaction maps, we find that &gt;40% of human C2H2-ZFPs are significantly enriched at LRI anchors. Depletion of a subset of such C2H2-ZFPs is associated with altered expression of genes linked by the LRIs they occupy. To investigate whether protein–protein interactions (PPIs) contribute to this pattern, we generated a large-scale interactome using affinity purification-mass spectrometry, encompassing 345 C2H2-ZFPs, and LUMIER binary interaction assays for 204 C2H2-ZFPs, revealing extensive connectivity among the family members. Integrative analysis of PPIs, ChIP-seq, and chromatin interaction datasets revealed that interacting C2H2-ZFP pairs are significantly co-enriched at LRIs and frequently localize to the same or opposing loop anchors. Finally, we show that ∼35% of LRI-associated C2H2-ZFP binding sites overlap somatic mutations in cancer genomes. Together, our results reveal a widespread network of C2H2-ZFP interactions at chromatin loop anchors, providing an important resource for understanding chromatin organization.

### Exploring the bistable equilibrium of methylated CpG DNA recognition by the MBD2 protein
**게재일**: 2026-10-19 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag952/8937190?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Methyl-CpG binding domain 2 (MBD2) is a critical epigenetic regulator that selectively binds methylated CpG dinucleotides, key marks controlling gene regulation and chromatin organization. Understanding the interactions and conformational dynamics underlying this high selectivity is essential to elucidate MBD2’s regulatory role. Here, using extensive classical MD simulations, we explored the formation of the MBD2–mCpG recognition complex. By initially positioning MBD2 one base pair downstream of its target, we observed its transition to the target site within microseconds. Notably, upon binding, MBD2 adopts two distinct stable conformations: a primary state closely resembling the X-ray crystal structure, and a secondary state of reduced affinity that nevertheless retains comparable selectivity for mCpG. Our results establish S189 as a key macro-switch; loss of its interaction with the methylcytosine backbone shifts the equilibrium toward the secondary state. This is corroborated by MD simulations of the S189A mutant, which preferentially adopts the secondary state-like conformation. Complementary NMR experiments confirm that S189A mutation does not alter mCpG selectivity, while fluorescence polarization measurements reveal a reduced binding affinity, consistent with our MD simulations results. Together, these findings indicate that MBD2 binding to methylated CpG involves a bistable equilibrium, providing new insights into how high affinity and adaptability are balanced in epigenetic recognition. In a broader context, our findings suggest that such alternative bound-state equilibria may represent an inherent feature of specific protein–DNA complexes.

### Highly effective viral RdRP translocation hindrance by the remdesivir nucleotide
**게재일**: 2026-10-19 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag957/8937189?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Nucleoside/nucleotide analogs (NAs) typically interfere with substrate selection and catalysis steps of polymerase nucleotide addition cycle (NAC), thereby affecting polymerase catalytic efficiency. In contrast, their interference with post-chemistry translocation is rarely considered in rational design, partly due to a lack of direct and quantitative evidence for such interference. Remdesivir (RDV) is an adenosine analog with broad-spectrum antiviral activity against diverse RNA virus families and has been approved for COVID-19 treatment. RDV is capable of inducing a characteristic “delayed” intervention, wherein the interference occurs several NACs after its incorporation. By combining X-ray crystallography, quench-flow kinetics, and molecular dynamics simulations, we show that the composite rate of RDV translocation from product strand position −3 to −4 and subsequent catalysis is at least 100-fold slower than that of adenosine in an enterovirus RdRP. However, mutating the corresponding roadblock residue serine417 to glycine largely abolishes this difference. Nonetheless, the incorporated RDV nucleotide can still alter the free-energy landscape even in the S417G mutant complex, enabling the capture of an over-translocated state. Our work demonstrates translocation-related intervention as an effective and broadly applicable polymerase intervention strategy in NA rational design.
