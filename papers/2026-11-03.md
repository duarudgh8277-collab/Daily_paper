### Physical determinants of reliable RNA–ligand binding prediction revealed by P-RISE
**게재일**: 2026-11-02 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1062/9006198?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract RNA-targeted small-molecule discovery is challenged by the scarcity of experimentally characterized RNA–ligand complexes and by the limited transferability of current computational approaches across chemically diverse systems. Here we present P-RISE, a physics-grounded framework for RNA–ligand binding prediction, and use it to identify the physical determinants underlying reliable performance. Systematic evaluation of force fields, ionic environments, dielectric treatments, and entropy corrections reveals three coupled requirements: preservation of RNA-binding pocket integrity under ion-sensitive conditions; an effective electrostatic error cancellation regime enabled by an elevated internal dielectric constant; and an empirically observed coupling between entropy correction and solvent modeling. Integrating these features, P-RISE provides stable affinity ranking, physics-grounded delineation of its physicochemical applicability domain, accurate metal ion site localization, and improved binding pose discrimination relative to established RNA docking and scoring approaches. Crucially, benchmarking against AlphaFold3 reveals that co-folding models, optimized for structure generation rather than energetic ranking, benefit from an explicit physics-based evaluation layer. Furthermore, diverse machine-learning comparisons demonstrate that purely data-driven methods lack stability and generalization in this data-poor regime. Ultimately, P-RISE provides a mechanistic understanding of RNA–ligand modeling, serving as a principled physical baseline that defines reliable prediction boundaries, and guides future data-driven integration.

### RNA viruses can exploit self-cleaving ribozymes for translation initiation
**게재일**: 2026-11-02 · [원문 링크](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/doi/10.1093/nar/gkag1106/9006196?rss=1)

**Abstract (EN)**: Abstract Small self-cleaving ribozymes are catalytic RNAs discovered in viroid-like agents, which are replicating circular RNAs (circRNAs) postulated as relics of a prebiotic RNA world. In the last decade, however, small ribozymes have also been detected across the tree of life, from bacterial to human genomes, and more recently, in unusual circRNA viruses. Here we report the conserved occurrence of diverse small ribozymes within the linear genomes of typical single- and double-stranded RNA viruses infecting fungi and plants. We identified type I hammerhead ribozymes in the 5′-UTRs of chrysovirids and fusarivirids, displaying self-cleaving activity in vitro and in vivo . Similar hammerhead, as well as hepatitis delta and twister ribozymes, occur in diverse megabirna-, hypo-, fusagra-, toti-, or tombus-like viruses. The ribozymes appear as isolated motifs within the UTRs or as tandem pairs that encompass small RNA segments (186–399 nt) resembling Zetavirus-like sequences. In vivo characterization of the 5′-UTR from a fungal chrysovirus revealed that its RNA self-cleaving activity is essential for protein translation initiation. Analogous experiments in plants with diverse ribozyme motifs confirmed that self-cleaving activity alone can induce cap-independent translation. We conclude that RNA self-cleaving activity, historically linked to the rolling circle replication of viroid-like circRNAs, appears to be co-opted by some linear RNA viruses for translational roles.
